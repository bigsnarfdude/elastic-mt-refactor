Which collision rule actually builds the microtubule array?

Sprint S1 — rule-ablation matrix on the elastic-mt cortical-MT simulator

2026-07-05 · weekend sprint · 25 runs on nigel

Knock out one collision rule at a time and watch the global order parameter. The surprise: zippering's explicit "rotate-to-align" step is not what aligns the array. Whether a colliding microtubule survives is. That reproduces the founding CorticalSim result from the other direction — by ablation instead of by construction.

TL;DR

  • Harness is trustworthy. The two anchor conditions reproduce the prior v0.2 numbers exactly (baseline S₂lw 0.854, no_zipper 0.568). Sanity gate passed before reading anything else.
  • Survival, not alignment, drives order. no_zipper and zipper_to_cross differ in one thing — whether a shallow-angle collision kills the MT or lets it cross and live. That one bit flips the array from disordered+sparse to ordered+dense.
  • The counterintuitive winner is real. zipper_to_cross reaches global order 0.892 — higher than baseline (0.361). Trajectory rises smoothly and 5 seeds converge (spread shrinks 0.092→0.005). Checked; not a metric artifact.
  • One condition was infeasible. always_cross never removes any MT → density and collision cost diverge. Runs hit 13–15 GB and 6–8 CPU-hours without finishing the first hour. Killed. That runaway is the result for that cell.
  • Read s2_global, not s2_region_lw. The per-region measure runs inflated across most conditions (up to a 0.65 gap). Global order is the one to trust.

The setup

Plant cortical microtubules start as a disordered tangle and self-organize into a globally aligned array — that alignment is what steers cell-wall growth. The field's models build this order from a few local collision rules: shallow-angle hits zipper (co-align), steep hits trigger induced catastrophe (the MT dies back), and new MTs nucleate.

The question for this sprint is the mechanistic-interpretability version of "which gene does what": treat the simulator as the object of study and ablate one rule at a time, then read the global order parameter S₂. It's activation-patching made literal on a hand-built simulator — and it runs entirely inside our own elastic-mt fork, with no dependency on the other groups' engines.

Two S₂ flavors get reported everywhere below. S₂lw is a length-weighted average over sub-regions (the v0.2 convention, comparable to prior numbers but biased upward at low per-region counts). S₂global pools the whole array — the honest "is it actually aligned" measure. Where they disagree, the array is not as ordered as the regional number claims.

Sanity gate — PASS. baseline S₂lw 0.854 and no_zipper 0.568 land on the v0.2 benchmark. The extraction and the ablation wiring are correct, so the rest of the table means something.
H1 — survival, not the alignment step, is the causal engine — CONFIRMED. no_zipper (removes align and adds ~50% death to shallow collisions) and zipper_to_cross (removes align only; shallow → cross and survive) differ in exactly one variable. That single bit flips the array from disordered+sparse (global 0.072, density 364) to ordered+dense (global 0.892, density 1842). The alignment rule itself isn't the engine — differential survival of colliding MTs plus steep-angle catastrophe pruning is.
Artifact check on the 0.892 — real, not a glitch. The trajectory rises smoothly and saturates (0.45→0.72→0.81→plateau) in all 5 seeds, and seed spread shrinks from 0.092 at 1h to 0.005 at 10h — independent seeds converging on one equilibrium, which noise can't fake. Its biggest single step is proportionally smaller than baseline's own jumps.
H2 — trust s2_global, not s2_region_lw — SUPPORTED. The decisive case is always_catas: global 0.057 (isotropic) yet lw 0.708. Nothing makes an array 71% locally ordered while 6% ordered array-wide — the per-region measure is simply inflated at low counts. zipper_to_cross is the one condition where the two agree (gap 0.02), confirming its order is genuinely array-wide.
always_cross — infeasible, and that's the finding. With no removal rule at all, MT count grows without bound; O(N²) collision cost explodes. Five runs pinned 13–15 GB each and 6–8 CPU-hours without clearing the first snapshot, saturating the host — killed. It's the clean proof that some density limiter is load-bearing.
Unscoped oddity, flagged not folded in. no_induced_catas (steep catastrophe removed, zippering intact) ends at S₂lw 0.362 — below no_zipper's 0.568. Removing steep catastrophe alone seems to hurt order more than removing zippering alone. Not part of the requested decomposition; needs its own check before anyone leans on it.

What it means

The no_zipper / zipper_to_cross pair isolates one variable cleanly — does a shallow-colliding microtubule live or die — and that variable alone decides whether the whole array orders. So the model's array-wide nematic order isn't produced by the explicit "rotate to align" rule at all. It comes from differential survival: MTs aligned with the local majority survive collisions, steep-angle induced catastrophe prunes the rest, and the population converges on one orientation.

That's the founding CorticalSim result (Tindemans / Deinum / Mulder — collision-induced catastrophe, not zippering, as the primary alignment mechanism), recovered here independently by a five-condition ablation instead of by citing it. This is reproduction-and-quantification, not a new claim — which is exactly the contribution that earns a seat in the cross-engine comparison: a "which-rule-drives-what" table other groups can hold their own engines against.
