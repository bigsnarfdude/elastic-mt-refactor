TL;DR
  • The no_zipper vs zipper_to_cross split isolates survival from alignment. Removing survival (no_zipper: shallow collisions die) collapses both order and density. Removing only the explicit alignment step (zipper_to_cross: shallow collisions cross over and live) barely dents order — global order actually comes out higher than baseline. Survival + steep-collision catastrophe is the causal driver, not the alignment step itself. This reproduces the founding CorticalSim (Tindemans/Deinum/Mulder) result by ablation.
  • s2_region_lw runs inflated relative to s2_global in 4 of 5 conditions — most starkly always_catas: lw 0.71 vs global 0.06 (near-isotropic). No real mechanism makes the array 71% locally ordered while 6% ordered array-wide, so s2_global is the measure to trust. zipper_to_cross is the one condition where lw ≈ global (gap 0.02) — the fingerprint of order that's genuinely array-wide, not a per-region artifact.
  • Artifact check on zipper_to_cross's high s2_global (0.89, above baseline's 0.36): the trajectory rises smoothly across all 5 seeds and the seed spread shrinks from std 0.09 at 1h to std 0.005 at 10h — a real, reproducible convergence, not a metric jump. Verdict: real effect.
  • always_cross (6th condition) is absent: infeasible at 10 sim-hr — density runaway, no MT ever removed, hit 13–15 GB RAM / 6–8 CPU-hr without finishing the first 1-hr snapshot. Killed. Qualitative result only.

Key Findings

Confirmed — survival, not alignment, drives global order

no_zipper (removes zippering AND kills ~50% of shallow collisions) ends disordered and sparse: s2_lw 0.568, s2_global 0.072, density 364. zipper_to_cross (removes only the explicit alignment step; shallow collisions cross over and the MT survives) ends ordered and dense: s2_lw 0.914, s2_global 0.892, density 1842 — denser than baseline. Since these two conditions differ only in whether colliding MTs survive, the surviving-MT population plus steep-collision induced catastrophe is what builds global order, not zippering's explicit angular-correction step. This reproduces the founding CorticalSim (Tindemans/Deinum/Mulder) result by ablation, not as a new discovery.

Verified — zipper_to_cross's high s2_global is real, not an artifact

Before trusting that removing alignment can raise global order above baseline, the trajectory was inspected directly. zipper_to_cross s2_global rises smoothly in all 5 seeds (mean 0.45 at 1h → 0.72 at 2h → 0.81 at 3h → plateau 0.87–0.89 by 5h), a classic saturating growth curve, and the seed-to-seed spread shrinks monotonically from std 0.092 at 1h to 0.005 at 10h — independent seeds converging on the same equilibrium. Largest single-step jump is ~10% of the total rise, no larger (in fact proportionally smaller) than baseline's own step-to-step noise. Verdict: real effect.

s2_region_lw runs inflated — trust s2_global

Ranking the lw−global gap across all 5 conditions settles which metric to trust: always_catas gap 0.65 (lw 0.708, global 0.057), no_zipper gap 0.50, baseline gap 0.49, no_induced_catas gap 0.28, zipper_to_cross gap 0.02. always_catas is decisive: an array that is 6% ordered array-wide cannot really be 71% locally ordered by any physical mechanism, so the lw estimator is inflated there, not detecting real local domains — and by extension likely inflated in baseline and no_induced_catas too, which show the same pattern at smaller magnitude. zipper_to_cross's near-zero gap is the one case where the two metrics agree, i.e. its order really is array-wide.

Unscoped observation — no_induced_catas is the most disordered condition tested

Not part of the original decomposition, but notable: no_induced_catas (steep-collision catastrophe removed, zippering intact) ends at s2_lw 0.362 — lower than even no_zipper's 0.568, despite retaining the alignment step. Removing steep-collision catastrophe alone appears to hurt order more than removing zippering alone. Flagging for follow-up rather than folding into the headline claim, since it wasn't part of the requested decomposition and needs its own dedicated check.

What This Means

The no_zipper / zipper_to_cross pair is a clean natural experiment: both remove the explicit "rotate this MT to align with what it hit" rule, and they differ in exactly one thing — what happens to an MT after a shallow-angle collision. no_zipper kills it (roughly half the time); zipper_to_cross lets it cross over and continue. That single difference is enough to flip the outcome from disordered+sparse to ordered+dense, matching baseline's density and exceeding baseline's global order.

That means the alignment step itself is not doing the causal work of building array-wide nematic order. What's doing the work is which MTs get to keep existing. Surviving MTs accumulate, collide more, and steep-angle collisions still trigger catastrophe — pruning the population toward whichever orientation is locally dominant. Zippering's explicit rotation looks, by this ablation, more like a local nudge on top of a process whose real engine is differential survival.

This is a reproduction of an already-published result (Tindemans/Deinum/Mulder's CorticalSim finding that collision-induced catastrophe, not explicit zippering, is the primary alignment mechanism), arrived at here independently via a 5-condition ablation rather than by citing it directly. Worth keeping in mind before treating the zipper_to_cross-above-baseline global-order number as surprising: it's consistent with that prior result, not a departure from it.
